    How the LEDs get power

    Power reaches the strip at the inside corner where the stem meets the band under the screen. That corner has two 6 mm wire holes, and the bare wall there stays free of LEDs. Use the LED power view to see it from behind.

    HoleWiresGoes to
    Band side, 5 mm right of the corner5V, ground and dataStart of the strip, running right along the underside of the band
    Stem side, 5 mm below the corner5V and ground onlyFar end of the strip, after it has gone all the way around

    Both ends get power so the far LEDs stay bright. The data wire connects only at the start. In the viewer, red is 5V, black is ground, green is data and orange is 12V.

    How the screen connects

    The panel is 194 × 119 × 5 mm, and 11 mm with its rear circuit board. It sits rotated 180°, so its HDMI and power ports face down toward the Pi. Use the Screen wiring view to see it from behind.

    How it goes together

    1. Cut the strip into 12 sections, one for each straight wall, and stick them to the channel wall. Solder short 3-wire jumpers across the 11 corners, following the arrows on the strip. The two ends finish at the wire holes beside the stem.
    2. Join the shell sections with the stem splice and 4 M3 screws, then glue on the diffusers.
    3. Hook each speaker's two front tabs under the pads, drivers facing the grille, then lower it onto the two rear posts and fit 2 M3 × 12 mm screws. Run a screwdriver in along the inside with the cover off. Do this before fitting the screen.
    4. Set the screen in from behind, rotated 180° so its ports face down, and screw on the 4 corner clips. The picture is flipped back in software.
    5. Assemble the Pi in its case, using the kit's extra brass spacers on top. Set it in its corner stops on the upper cover and fit the three brackets. Add the converter and level shifter. Set the USB dongle in its cradle, screw the strap over it, and connect it to the Pi with its short USB cable. Then route and zip-tie the wires.
    6. Fit the power jack through the foot, connect everything and test it.
    7. Screw on both covers. The upper cover bridges the shell joint for strength.
    8. Hang it on 2 wall screws, 201 mm apart, using the keyholes.

    How it's designed

    • Power cord: It enters through the bottom of the foot and hangs straight down the wall.
    • Wires: They run up the stem, then along a channel above the speakers, behind the screen. Each route is checked as a 3 mm bundle.
    • Speakers: Two rear screws and two front hooks hold each one. The grille sits about 9 mm below the speaker face, and a 16 mm gap between the speakers gives their cables room.
    • Raspberry Pi: The N300 case has no mounting holes underneath, so three printed brackets hold it from its top spacers, with corner stops around the case. Its fins face the back cover vents.
    • USB dongle: It has no mounting holes, so a low cradle and screwed strap hold it, with its 4-pin header facing the speakers.
    • LEDs: 72 LEDs sit on the outer wall of a 10 mm deep channel, facing out, about 6 mm behind the diffuser. Each corner has room for a soldered jumper.
    • Cooling: Cool air enters low, through the bottom of the foot, the stem walls, the underside and the lower right wall. It rises past the electronics and leaves through the top wall and upper side walls, so the airflow doesn't depend on the gap behind the sign.
    • Servicing: The covers come off with screws, so the wiring stays reachable. The diffusers are glued on.

    Send when parts arrive

    • Screen: exact port positions on the rear board. They're scaled from a photo
    • Speakers, which you already have: length, width, thickness, tab hole size and spacing, and how far the tabs sit from the face
    • Converter: length, width, thickness and mounting holes
    • USB dongle, which you already have: total length, body length, width and thickness
    • Power jack: hole size and thread
    • LED strip: width, LEDs per meter, total length and thickness
    • Pi in its case: length, width, thickness and top-of-USB height

    Small hardware

    • 32 M3 × 10 mm self-tapping screws: 22 for the covers, 4 for the splice, 2 for the dongle strap and 4 for the Pi brackets
    • 4 M3 × 8 mm self-tapping screws for the screen clips. Don't go longer, or they will reach the front face
    • 4 M3 × 12 mm self-tapping screws, for the speakers
    • 4 M2.5 × 6 mm screws, for the level shifter board. The Pi case kit includes its own M2.5 screws and spacers
    • 10 small zip ties, 3.6 mm wide or narrower, for the 10 clips on the back cover
    • 2 wall screws with heads under 9 mm, shafts under 5 mm
    • Thin wire for 33 corner jumper wires, 3 at each of 11 corners. Stranded 24 AWG or thinner bends best
    • 4 stick-on rubber bumpers, about 8 mm tall, to hold the back off the wall
    • CA glue for the diffusers, plus thin foam tape for the dongle strap
